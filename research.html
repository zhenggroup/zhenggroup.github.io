Research

We study electrochemical interfaces under operating conditions, connecting surface chemistry and electrode evolution with catalytic activity, product selectivity, and durability. Our recent work centers on water electrolysis, ammonia oxidation, and the reliability of electrochemical measurements.

Operando Interfaces and Reaction Selectivity

We combine electrochemical measurements with operando Raman and UV–vis spectroscopy, differential electrochemical mass spectrometry (DEMS), and electrochemical atomic force microscopy (EC-AFM) to examine changing interfaces. Correlating surface observations with gaseous and dissolved products helps connect electrode chemistry to reaction pathways.

Our ammonia-oxidation studies on NiOOH and RuO₂ examine how potential, electrolyte composition, and competing oxygen chemistry change product selectivity. Collaborative work on nickel phosphides follows the catalyst state during hydrogen evolution, distinguishing the working surface from changes that occur after the potential is removed.

Degradation, Electrode Stability, and Catalyst Design

We investigate how surface reconstruction, dissolution, and changing electrical properties contribute to performance loss. Time-resolved electrochemical impedance analysis follows the evolution of interfacial capacitance, resistance, and relaxation behavior alongside current and potential. Studies of RuO₂ and Pt/C connect these measurements with degradation during oxygen and hydrogen electrocatalysis.

We also examine the electrode beneath the catalyst. Studies of flexible indium tin oxide (ITO) and fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) combine electrical, optical, and morphological measurements to identify substrate degradation and its influence on operando signals. Materials-design studies explore Bi-modified copper oxide for glucose oxidation and resin-derived carbon-supported catalysts for oxygen evolution.

Core Questions

  • Which interfacial changes accompany activation, gradual decay, or irreversible failure?

Quantitative Electrochemistry and Reliable Evaluation

We develop measurement and analysis methods that account for the changing state of an electrode. Our work on uncompensated resistance combines time- and potential-resolved measurements with state-matched, point-by-point iR correction. This approach addresses errors that arise when a single resistance value is applied throughout a changing experiment.

We examine when charge-transfer resistance can support an interpretation of intrinsic kinetics, considering adsorption, transport, active-site normalization, and evolving interfaces. Related studies assess counter-electrode choice, cell configuration, and the experimental and interpretive limits of operando measurements. Practical protocols and open analysis tools make these considerations easier to apply and reproduce.

Core Questions

  • How should resistance, current, and potential be paired when an electrode changes during a measurement?
  • When does a fitted resistance reflect interfacial kinetics, and what additional evidence is needed?
  • How do counter electrodes, cell geometry, and measurement protocols influence the conclusions?
